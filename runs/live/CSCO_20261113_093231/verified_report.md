## 5. Investment Recommendation

**Recommendation: Hold.**

The deciding number: Free cash flow growth, FY2026 vs FY2025: **-3.92%** ((14,177.0 - 1,410.0) / (14,193.0 - 905.0) - 1; us-gaap:NetCashProvidedByUsedInOperatingActivities and us-gaap:PaymentsToAcquirePropertyPlantAndEquipment, 10-K 0000858877-26-000132, filed 2026-09-02).

Cisco's earnings are accelerating. FY2026 GAAP diluted EPS grew **30.59%** to $3.33, and Q1 FY2027 GAAP diluted EPS grew **58.33%** on revenue up **24.42%**, led by Networking up 43% and hyperscaler AI demand that management now expects to reach $9B in FY27. At a **P/E of 30.6**, however, the stock already prices in roughly the FY2026 rate of EPS growth. Cash has not followed earnings: free cash flow fell **3.92%** to **$12,767.0M** while net income grew **30.32%**, because cash from operations was flat (**-0.11%**) and additions to property and equipment rose **55.80%**. The balance sheet is also absorbing the AI build-out: inventory rose 80%, inventory purchase commitments rose 126%, and Q1 GAAP product gross margin fell to 61.8% from 64.5%. Strong returns (return on average equity of **27.32%**) and falling leverage (debt to equity of **0.455**) limit the downside, so the case is Hold rather than Sell.

What would change it: We would move to Buy if free cash flow starts growing with earnings, which would show that the inventory build is converting to cash at a steady gross margin. We would move to Sell if inventory and purchase commitments keep rising while gross margin keeps falling below the Q1 GAAP level of 63.7%.

Insight: At the end of FY2026, Cisco's inventory was up 80% and its inventory purchase commitments with contract manufacturers and suppliers were up 126%, which signals a large forward supply build behind the AI demand that the release's first paragraph does not mention.
