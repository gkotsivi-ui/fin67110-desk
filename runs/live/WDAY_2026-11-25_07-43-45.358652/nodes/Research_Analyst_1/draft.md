## Overview

This review tests Workday's growth claims using only the FY2026 10-K Business and MD&A sections and the Q3 FY2027 earnings release. Workday describes itself as an enterprise AI platform for HCM, financial management, spend management and planning. It serves more than 11,500 [10-K.txt:L247] organizations with more than 75 million [10-K.txt:L248] users under contract, including more than 65% [10-K.txt:L248] of the companies on the Fortune list of the largest U.S. companies. Revenue is mostly recurring: subscription services made up approximately 92% [10-K.txt:L1048] of total revenues in FY2026. Total revenues were $9.552 billion [XBRL tag RevenueFromContractWithCustomerExcludingAssessedTax, period end 2026-01-31, filing 0001327811-26-000014] in FY2026, up from $8.446 billion [XBRL tag RevenueFromContractWithCustomerExcludingAssessedTax, period end 2025-01-31, filing 0001327811-26-000014] in FY2025. Subscription services revenues reached $8.8 billion [10-K.txt:L1096], up 14% [10-K.txt:L1096]. Expansion within existing customers accounted for approximately 60% [10-K.txt:L1096] of the subscription revenue increase, and newly added customers accounted for the remaining 40% [10-K.txt:L1096]. Neither the extracted 10-K sections nor the earnings release give a customer-concentration figure, so revenue concentration is not established by the supplied evidence.

**Demand durability: supported.** The 10-K reports a gross revenue retention rate of approximately 97% [10-K.txt:L1098] as of January 31, 2026. Total subscription revenue backlog was $28.1 billion [10-K.txt:L1101], with $8.8 billion [10-K.txt:L1101] expected to be recognized over the following year. In Q3 FY2027, subscription revenues grew 12.8% [earnings_release_Ex99-1.txt:L14] year-over-year, and the portion of subscription revenue backlog due within the next year grew 12.3% [earnings_release_Ex99-1.txt:L24]. The MD&A also flags risks to this view: lengthening sales cycles in net new opportunities and reduced growth in headcount-level commitments when existing customers renew.

## Market Positioning and Growth Outlook

**Competitive position: mixed.** Workday names Oracle and SAP as its primary competitors and says it competes favorably on factors such as customer satisfaction, breadth of functionality and AI capability. The supplied materials contain no segment results, so a segment-level advantage is not established by the supplied evidence. Operating measures partly support the claim. Non-GAAP operating margin was 29.6% [10-K.txt:L1221] in FY2026, compared with 25.9% [10-K.txt:L1221] in FY2025. In Q3 FY2027 it was 31.4% [earnings_release_Ex99-1.txt:L18], compared with 28.5% [earnings_release_Ex99-1.txt:L18] a year earlier. However, GAAP operating margin was only 7.5% [10-K.txt:L1220] in FY2026, and the company booked approximately $303 million [10-K.txt:L1039] in restructuring costs. Gartner Leader designations are third-party opinions, not operating measures, so I give them no weight in this assessment.

AI adoption is an early sign of advantage. More than 2,200 [earnings_release_Ex99-1.txt:L47] core customers have purchased at least one AI product, up nearly 25% [earnings_release_Ex99-1.txt:L47] from the prior quarter. Management also says AI products drove nearly $150 million [earnings_release_Ex99-1.txt:L36] in new ACV in the quarter. That figure comes from a CEO quote rather than the financial statements, so I treat it as a management claim.

**Growth execution: mixed.** Investment rose unevenly in FY2026. Costs of subscription services grew 21% [10-K.txt:L1147], driven by third-party hosted infrastructure, while product development grew only 2% [10-K.txt:L1153] and sales and marketing grew 8% [10-K.txt:L1156]. Workday paid $1.1 billion [10-K.txt:L1033] for Paradox and made several other AI acquisitions, and it expects capital expenditures of approximately $270 million [10-K.txt:L1397] in FY2027. At the same time, it spent $2.9 billion [10-K.txt:L1036] on share repurchases in FY2026 and issued $2.5 billion [earnings_release_Ex99-1.txt:L71] of senior notes in Q3 FY2027. Management raised FY2027 subscription revenue guidance to $9.958 billion [earnings_release_Ex99-1.txt:L103], implying growth of 13% [earnings_release_Ex99-1.txt:L103]. However, Q4 FY2027 guidance implies subscription growth of only 10% [earnings_release_Ex99-1.txt:L97], which points to slowing growth.

## Future Developments and Strategic Recommendations

The catalysts to watch are new AI products and pricing: Workday Build, Workday Data Cloud, Flex Credits pricing and the partnerships with Anthropic and Accenture. The 10-K itself warns that Workday has limited experience pricing Flex Credits contracts and that AI monetization remains uncertain. Investors should track whether AI purchasing (currently more than 2,200 [earnings_release_Ex99-1.txt:L47] core customers) turns into subscription growth above the Q4 FY2027 guide of 10% [earnings_release_Ex99-1.txt:L97].

The backlog is also worth watching. Total subscription revenue backlog was $27.548 billion [earnings_release_Ex99-1.txt:L24] in Q3 FY2027, compared with $28.1 billion [10-K.txt:L1101] at fiscal year-end. Workday describes Q4 as its seasonally heaviest quarter for signings and renewals, so the year-end figure will show whether total backlog growth picks up again.

**What would change my view.** My strongest conclusion is that demand is durable. It would be challenged if the next 10-K reports gross revenue retention below the current 97% [10-K.txt:L1098]. A lower figure would mean the recurring base itself is shrinking, not just slowing. It would also undercut the MD&A's claim that existing customers drive approximately 60% [10-K.txt:L1096] of the subscription revenue increase.

Insight: In Q3 FY2027, subscription revenue backlog due within the next year grew 12.3% [earnings_release_Ex99-1.txt:L24] while total subscription revenue backlog grew only 6.1% [earnings_release_Ex99-1.txt:L24], so contracted revenue beyond the next year is growing more slowly than near-term revenue, a duration issue the release's headline revenue figures do not show.
